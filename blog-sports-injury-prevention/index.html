5 ways athletes can prevent common sports injuries

September 20265 min readDr. Satish, OT Nest

Most of the sports injuries we treat — hamstring strains, ankle sprains, ACL tears, shoulder impingement — are not freak accidents. They cluster around a handful of predictable risk factors, most of which are fixable with a few weeks of deliberate preparation.

1. Warm up like you mean it

A proper warm-up is dynamic, not static: leg swings, lunges, and sport-specific movement patterns at increasing intensity, for at least 10 minutes. This raises tissue temperature and primes the nervous system in a way that standing and touching your toes does not.

2. Train the muscles that get forgotten

Hamstrings, glutes, and the muscles around the shoulder blade are chronically undertrained relative to the muscles athletes can see in the mirror. Weak hamstrings are one of the strongest predictors of a hamstring strain; weak glutes are linked to ACL injury risk in cutting sports.

3. Respect load management

A sharp jump in training volume or intensity — going from occasional play to daily training, or suddenly doubling your running distance — is one of the biggest injury risk factors there is. Build volume up by no more than roughly 10% a week.

4. Don't play through joint pain

Muscle soreness after effort is normal. Sharp, localised joint pain, or pain that changes how you move, is not something to push through. Continuing to load an already-irritated structure is how a minor niggle becomes a multi-week injury.

5. Build in a real off-season

Constant year-round competition in a single sport, especially for younger athletes, is strongly linked to overuse injuries. A few weeks of lower-intensity cross-training gives overloaded tissues a genuine chance to recover.

Already carrying a niggle? A movement screen can pick up the asymmetries and weaknesses behind recurring strains before they become a serious injury — well worth it ahead of a season or a big event.
